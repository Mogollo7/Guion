# Editing reference: voice, music, cuts

## Duration from words
| rate (words/s) | feel | use |
|---|---|---|
| 2.4–2.6 | calm, formal | worst-case budget, voice-over for older audiences |
| 2.8–3.0 | lively presenter | default for pitches and class videos |
| 3.1–3.3 | fast, still clear | when a hard limit forces it; rehearse first |

Add 0.2–0.4 s of breath between lines. A 3-minute limit holds about 390–480 words at 2.6–3.0 words/s, minus the intro
and outro.

## A-roll / B-roll split (talking head + plates)
- First line of each speaker: 40–50 % on camera (min ~2 s) so people meet them.
- Following lines: 20–30 % on camera (min ~1.2 s), then the plate.
- Product or prototype demos: on camera ~1 s, then the demo carries the line. A small picture-in-picture of the
  presenter keeps "everyone appears" satisfied.
- If the plate's key animation needs more time than the line gives, shorten the on-camera part.

## Music grid
- Tempo of launch and promo films: 108–136 BPM, median ~123 (earshot measurements on 7 professional films).
- At 120 BPM and 30 fps: 1 beat = 15 frames, 1 bar = 60 frames. A promo built in multiples of bars stays in sync
  with almost no effort.
- Snap cuts within half a beat. A cut 1–2 frames early feels tighter than one late.
- Confidence < 0.5: the tracker did not lock. Cut on phrases and check by watching.

## Accents
- 1.2–1.7 salient accents per second in pro films, over a dense music bed. Accent the reveal, the logo, the key UI
  action and the final line.
- Before a big hit, the music can step down for 0.3–0.5 s and come back on the hit.

## Subtitles
- 1–2 lines, at most ~42 characters per line, about 8 words per cue.
- Keep each cue on screen for its words at the speaking rate; do not split a name across cues.
- Per-speaker SRTs starting at 0 are easier to align than one global file when clips are recorded separately.
